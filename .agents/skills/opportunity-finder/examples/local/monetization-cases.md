# Local derived examples

These are Harness-authored examples, not examples from the source repository.

## Vague programmer

Input: “I am a programmer but do not know what to do to make money.”

Review direction: Do not list AI products. Inventory recurring tasks and reachable groups from the user's last two jobs, then ask the user to document five observed workarounds and speak to three people performing the most costly one.

## Trend-led idea

Input: “AI short video is hot; I want to build an automated agent.”

Review direction: Treat “hot” and “creators need full automation” as assumptions. Investigate which production step creators repeatedly outsource, redo, or pay for; route the build impulse to `assumption-challenger` and `experiment-designer`.

## Content goal with strong founder fit

Input: “I am a programmer and want to turn real AI practice into content that
eventually earns money.”

Review direction: Treat technical familiarity and peer access as Investigation
Advantages, not evidence that technical peers are the best audience or payer.
Treat “content may suit me” as a hypothesis unless explicitly committed. Do not
rank H3, AI serial drama, and commerce video directly: H3 may be a topic or tool,
serial drama a format, and commerce a monetization structure or service
hypothesis. Normalize to observed monetization structures, then map those
components. If no direct Reality signal distinguishes the structures, request a
bounded comparison of value, payer, money-flow, resource, and failure patterns.
A single reversible content probe may explore audience interest with an unknown
bridge; a multi-day content series must explain which money-path unknown it will
reduce.
